# Scoring Knowledge Base

Use this reference to turn picture evidence into musical decisions. Treat it as a control system, not a style-name list.

## 1. Narrative Roles

A cue should perform at least one explicit job:

- orient place, period, culture, scale, or point of view;
- create expectation before the picture confirms it;
- reveal subtext that acting/dialogue does not state;
- contradict the surface to create irony, dread, denial, or emotional distance;
- bind montage, passage of time, or parallel action;
- transform a character/object motif as meaning changes;
- transfer rhythm between scenes;
- withhold music so source sound, performance, or shock owns the moment.

If removing the cue changes nothing, redesign or omit it.

## 2. Spotting Logic

For every cue, define:

```text
entry reason -> development question -> structural turn -> consequence -> exit reason
```

Prefer cue boundaries at changes of dramatic proposition, viewpoint, location, time, or agency. A cut alone is not a reason to start a cue.

Selective sync points:

- first reveal;
- irreversible choice;
- contact with consequence;
- recognition/reversal;
- title/product/logo arrival;
- comic punctuation;
- final visual or sonic button.

Use anticipation for menace/momentum, exact sync for proof/impact, delayed response for weight/grief, and continuous phrasing when the image needs flow.

## 3. Tempo And Edit Rhythm

Basic timing:

```text
quarter-note seconds = 60 / BPM
eighth-note seconds = 30 / BPM
bar seconds in 4/4 = 240 / BPM
bar seconds in 3/4 = 180 / BPM
bar seconds in 6/8 (two dotted-quarter pulses) = 120 / BPM
```

Use cut intervals to generate candidate pulse grids, then audition half-time, double-time, dotted, and triplet interpretations. Do not quantize the entire picture. Lock only the beats that need musical proof; let other cuts breathe across the phrase.

When a required hit falls off-grid, prefer this order:

1. change pickup/subdivision;
2. add or remove a beat/bar;
3. use a fermata, breath, break, or tempo ramp;
4. shift a phrase boundary;
5. make a small time edit;
6. regenerate the cue when form is fundamentally wrong.

## 4. Tension Controls

Translate emotion into multiple audible parameters:

| Need | Rhythm | Harmony | Register/timbre | Dynamics/space |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| stalking dread | unstable sparse pulse | pedal, semitone friction, withheld cadence | low-mid drone plus isolated high detail | narrow/dry foreground, distant tail |
| panic | compressed subdivisions, interruption | faster harmonic rhythm, clusters | rising register, abrasive transient | density surge, reduced headroom before break |
| intimate grief | weak or free pulse | suspended/added tones, slow harmonic rhythm | exposed solo, breath/noise, narrow range | close/dry, long controlled decay after phrase |
| earned hope | steady but not triumphant pulse | gradual consonance, open intervals | register opens, warmer family enters | stereo/room widens after proof |
| authority/inevitability | heavy periodicity, few attacks | pedal/open fifths, slow root motion | low register, coherent ensemble | strong body, restrained peak count |
| comic timing | setup rhythm plus interruption | expectation then sidestep | contrasting small/awkward timbre | dry punctuation, short tail |

Do not change every parameter at once. Preserve one identity while one or two variables transform.

## 5. Motif Design And Transformation

A motif may be an interval, contour, rhythm, harmony, timbre, pulse, or sound object. Keep it short enough to survive transformation.

Transform through:

- fragmentation or completion;
- inversion;
- augmentation/diminution;
- register migration;
- mode/harmonic-context change;
- acoustic-to-electronic transfer;
- solo-to-ensemble growth;
- dry source sound expanding into score;
- rhythmic identity surviving while pitches disappear.

Do not repeat an unchanged theme merely to label a character.

## 6. Orchestration Under Dialogue

Protect speech before reaching for a fader:

- thin the 1–4 kHz midrange during essential words;
- keep busy melody out of the speech register;
- reduce percussion density and long reverb tails;
- use low pulse, high air, sparse attacks, or stable drones around dialogue;
- reserve brass/choir/full-spectrum impacts for gaps;
- automate arrangement/stems first, sidechain ducking second;
- use pre-delay so reverb bloom begins after a transient or phrase.

For voice-led scenes, request stems such as rhythm, bass, harmony/pad, melody, percussion, and impacts.

## 7. Short-Form Cue Architectures

### 5–15 seconds

```text
identity seed -> one escalation -> one reveal/drop -> clear button or tail
```

Use one memorable musical object. Do not force verse/chorus form.

### 15–60 seconds

```text
orientation -> engine/motif -> contrast or subtraction -> decisive turn -> exit
```

Create contrast through subtraction before adding more layers.

### Trailer / showcase

Separate information beats from impact beats. Typical functions are hook, premise, escalation, false drop, reveal, aftermath, and title button; the exact order follows the cut.

### Emotional performance

Do not score the first visible tear/anger by default. Consider entering on resistance, recognition, or aftermath so music adds an unseen layer.

### Action

Let physical sound prove motion/contact. Music owns anticipation, macro pulse, reversals, and scale. Remove or thin music at decisive contact when the effect needs weight.

## 8. AI Music Generation Evaluation

Judge a generated cue in this order:

1. dramatic interpretation;
2. form and timing;
3. motif/identity;
4. dialogue-safe arrangement;
5. ending/button;
6. production artifacts;
7. editability and stems.

Regenerate when the interpretation, form, or ending is wrong. Edit when the performance is useful and only local timing/length needs repair.

## 9. Loudness And Delivery

LUFS is a measurement, not a quality target. ITU-R BS.1770 defines loudness/true-peak measurement; EBU R 128 builds broadcast normalization around it. EBU's broadcast reference is -23 LUFS, while web/social delivery often uses different house targets. Always follow the actual delivery specification.

For this skill's generic web defaults:

- 48 kHz, stereo, 24-bit PCM WAV;
- music-only around -18 LUFS integrated;
- full mix around -14 LUFS integrated;
- true peak at or below -1 dBTP;
- preserve dynamic contrast and dialogue foreground.

Do not use sparse SFX-bus integrated loudness as a primary balancing metric; set it by perspective and transient hierarchy, then measure the complete programme.
